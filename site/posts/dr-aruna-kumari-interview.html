✦ Meet the Expert Dr. Aruna Kumari Gynecologist

A leading gynecologist sharing expert insights on women's health changes after 45.

1. General Health Screenings
Q. How can woman reduce risk of gynecological problems as cancer?
Ans.

Cancer cervix is second most common in women next only to breast cancer. Screening for cervical cancer starts early during reproductive age around 20s after the woman becomes sexually active. Pap smear test should be done annually in young, sexually active women. The policy of universal screening for cervical cancer has brought down the incidence of invasive cervical cancer significantly. Through pap smear cancer cervix can be detected in the pre invasive stages and they are completely treatable when detected early.

"Research has shown the correlation of human papilloma virus (HPV) as the etiological factor for cancer cervix."

And therefore, HPV vaccine is administered universally to all girls prior to getting sexually active but preferable around 9-15 years.

Q. What health screenings should they prioritize at this stage [Pap smear, HPV, mammogram, bone density, etc.]?
Ans.

Breast cancer screening with mammogram, breast ultrasound starts around 40 years. Ideally both should be performed as the results are complimentary to each other. Most of the protocols suggest mammograms once in 3 years unless there are a strong family history or other risk factors. Prior to 40 years, women should be educated to perform self-examination correctly and report to family physician in case of any doubt.

Q. How often should women have pelvic exams after 40?
Ans.

Routine pelvic examination and pelvic ultrasound are not required unless there is a strong family history of ovarian cancer. However, pelvic ultrasound is not sensitive and cost effective as screening test.

Q. Does she need to consider genetic screen testing for breast, ovarian or uterine cancer?
Ans.

Breast, ovarian and colonic cancers are classified under familial group of cancers and have some familial predispositions. If two first degree relatives have a similar type of cancer, only then it puts the woman at a higher risk and genetic consultation is probably required but if random family members or close family members have totally different, unconnected cancers that does not increase the individual risk for that woman.

2. Hormonal & Menopause Health Issues
Q. How can women manage perimenopausal symptoms like hot-flushes or mood swings?
Ans.

Hot flushes and mood swings are primary due to estrogen deprivation due to ovarian aging. There are several herbal and other preparations to alleviate these symptoms only marginally. If the symptoms are severe enough to affect work performance and quality of life, estrogen replacement is to be considered.

Q. Should they consider HRT therapy, what are benefits and risks?
Ans.

Estrogen replacement effectively reverses all menopause related issues. Research has shown marginally increased risk of breast cancer with HRT. The overall risk is marginal, but it has to be informed to the patient prior to administering and treatment should be started after informed consent. She has to be kept under vigilance with annual follow ups and breast workups.

Q. Are there natural or lifestyle alternatives to manage menopause symptoms?
Ans.

Active lifestyle and daily exercise releases lots of endorphins (good hormones) and to some extent helps to minimize menopausal symptoms. Several herbal preparations are available in the market with questionable efficacy and safety.

3. Bone, Heart & Metabolic Health
Q. How can we prevent osteoporosis or bone density loss as we are getting old? Does Calcium and vitamin D supplements help?
Ans.

Menopause is associated with bone loss and susceptibility to falls and therefore fractures. The best way to maintain bone and muscle strength is through weight-bearing exercises and a healthy lifestyle.

Bone mineral density (BMD) estimations start only after 65 years of age, earlier only in the presence of other risk factors in the form of family history of osteoporosis, loss of height or use of medications which increase bone loss.

Q. How does gynecological health relate to cardiovascular changes with age?
Ans.

Estrogens are protective of the heart. After menopause the susceptibility to cardiovascular events equals that in men (higher risk after menopause). Hormone replacement therapy has been shown to decrease the risk significantly.

Q. Do lifestyle changes lower the risk of heart disease?
Ans.

Healthy diet and regular exercise maintaining ideal bodyweight have all been shown beyond doubt to lower the risk of heart disease.

4. Ovarian Health & Fertility
Q. What steps can be taken to ensure Ovarian health with age?
Ans.

Genetics plays a part in overall ovarian health. Healthy lifestyle, avoid smoking and minimizing alcohol intake would contribute to ovarian health.

Q. Are there significant changes in Fertility after 40 years?
Ans.

There is an exponential drop in fertility after 40 years. And the eggs that are produced after 40 years are similar in age to the chronological age of the woman and therefore are more subject to chromosomal mutations and other aging changes. Unlike in men where fresh spermatozoa are produced every 72 hours.

5. Pelvic Health & Incontinence
Q. What are the signs of pelvic floor dysfunction, and how can it be prevented to treat incontinence?
Ans.

Signs of pelvic floor dysfunction is usually because of pelvic floor trauma due to childbirth. When it happens, and the woman notices signs of incontinence, consult a gynecologist/urologist.

Q. What treatments are available for pelvic organ prolapse?
Ans.

Non operative options like pessaries are available to keep the organs in place. Operative treatments with artificial mesh/sling reinforce the weakened ligaments and reposition pelvic organs in place.

Q. What exercises are recommended for maintaining pelvic health?
Ans.

Performing Kegel exercises during postpartum period and thereafter avoiding constipation might help to maintain integrity of pelvic organ wellness.

6. Wellness and Emotional Health
Q. What are the specific lifestyle habits like diet, exercise, stress management etc. Women should adopt for optimal health in 40s and above?
Ans.

Healthy lifestyle in the form of balanced diet, regular exercise, yoga and meditation go a long way in establishing both physical and emotional health of a woman.

Q. What mental health supports or therapies would you recommend for someone experiencing these hormonal changes?
Ans.

Family support would go a long way to help the women experiencing menopausal symptoms when they are severe enough one must consider hormone replacement therapy or group of medicines called serotonin receptor inhibitors to be decided by the treating doctor.

Q. How do gynecological health changes affect overall wellness after 45?
Ans.

Blood lipids, bone and joints, Muscles and skin, cognition, urogenital — all of these respond to estrogens and therefore Estrogen deprivation affects all these organs and causes a variety of symptoms like irritability, depression, loss of libido, hot flashes, night sweats, vaginal dryness urinary urgency, insomnia dryness of skin and thus affect quality of life and overall well-being of women.

These above conversations with expert and her guidance would be great help for women to establish a preventive plan. Also help to monitor early changes that could identify issues, leading to better management and health outcomes.
